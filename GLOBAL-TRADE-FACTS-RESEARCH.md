# GLOBAL TRADE FACTS — RESEARCH REPORT

**Researched 2 October 2026.** For the Jericho website's interactive facts section.
No code has been written. This document is for review before anything is built.

Every figure below was fetched from a source during this research. Nothing is
recalled from memory, estimated or extrapolated. Where a figure could not be
verified, it says so and is listed under **D. GAPS** rather than filled in.

---

## STATUS KEY

Used in the tables and required on the page itself.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **M** | Measured / reported statistic from the source organisation |
| **P** | Preliminary — the source labels it provisional and expects revision |
| **E** | Estimate — the source states it is an estimate subject to revision |
| **F** | Forecast / projection — a prediction, not a record of what happened |
| **C** | Calculation — derived by arithmetic from other figures |

Your rule was to keep these distinct. Three of the headline facts are **E** or
**P**, not **M**, and the page must not present them as hard statistics.

## C. FACTS TO REJECT

Each of these would have made the page look better and should not be used.

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Container trade: 920 million TEU"** | The 920 million figure is **port handling**, not trade. It counts the same box again at every transhipment hub. Containerised seaborne *trade* is roughly 170 million TEU — about 5.4 times smaller. Presenting throughput as trade volume is the single most common error on pages like this. Keep the number, fix the title. |
| **"World merchandise trade was US$8.69 trillion in 2024"** | This appeared in a search result during research and is **wrong** by roughly a factor of three. The verified WTO figure is US$24.43 trillion for 2024 and US$26.26 trillion for 2025. Recorded here because it shows why nothing went on the page without being checked at source. |
| **"Global crude oil trade: well over 100 million barrels per day"** | Also produced by a search summary during research. It is impossible — total world petroleum supply is around 103 million b/d, so trade cannot exceed it. Discarded. |
| **Crude-oil-only seaborne trade volume** | No free authoritative full-year figure found. The EIA figure available (79.8 million b/d) is **first half of 2025**, not a full year, and covers **crude plus refined products**, not crude alone. Using it as "global crude oil trade" would be wrong twice over. See **D. GAPS**. |
| **A single "global commodity growth rate"** | You ruled this out and you were right. Tonnage, tonne-miles, production and trade value grew at different rates in different directions in 2025. Any combined figure would be manufactured. |
| **2025 and 2026–2030 seaborne trade growth (+0.5%, +2% a year)** | These are UNCTAD **forecasts**, not records. Available and quotable, but a forecast on a brochure page reads as a claim about the future. Recommend leaving out. |
| **"Total global commodity trade value" as a single USD figure** | Not a standard statistical category, exactly as you suspected. See **D. GAPS** for what I recommend instead. |
| **Jericho's own numbers of any kind** | Tonnes, shipments, suppliers, buyers, countries, years, transaction value. None exist in verified form and none will be invented. |
| **Chrome ore, ferrochrome, manganese, antimony volumes** | Assessed markets with no free authoritative public figures, consistent with what your own handover already established. Excluded per your instruction. |

## D. GAPS — what I could not verify

Reported rather than filled in, as instructed.

1. **Absolute USD value of fuels and mining products.** WTO publishes year-on-year
   growth by product group in its free reports, but keeps the absolute values by
   product group in its paid statistics database (`stats.wto.org`). I could not
   reach a figure I would stand behind. The World Bank's WITS world summary is
   JavaScript-rendered and returned no values either.
   **Recommendation:** present the verified *direction* instead — commodity trade
   value fell 4% in 2025 while total world trade rose 7% — which is a more
   interesting fact than the absolute number would have been, and is fully sourced.

2. **Crude-oil-only global trade, full year.** Not published free in a form that
   separates crude from refined products across a full year. **Recommendation:**
   either state the verified figure with its exact scope — "79.8 million barrels a
   day of crude oil and refined products moved by sea, first half of 2025, EIA" —
   or drop oil volume and use the oil tanker share of the world fleet instead,
   which is measured, full-year and unambiguous.

3. **Containerised seaborne trade volume (~170 million TEU, 2024).** The figure
   recurs in secondary sources but I could not trace it to a primary publication in
   this session. **Recommendation:** use the verified 920 million TEU port handling
   figure with the correct title, and leave the trade-volume figure out until it can
   be sourced properly.

4. **UNCTAD's own PDFs are unreachable from this environment.** Every
   `unctad.org` document returns HTTP 403, by direct fetch and by command line. The
   UNCTAD figures above come from UNCTAD's published content via the search index and
   secondary reporting, not from the PDF itself. They are internally consistent and
   repeated across independent sources, but **I have not read them off the original
   document**, and you should know that before they go on a page. If you can open
   `rmt2025ch1_en.pdf` in your own browser, the figures worth confirming are: 12,720
   million tonnes, 66,781 billion tonne-miles, 112,500 vessels, 2.44 billion dwt.

## E. THE MAP

You chose real seaborne trade flows between regions. One problem and one good
answer.

**The problem.** Region-to-region seaborne flow matrices are commercial products.
Clarksons and Kpler sell them; UNCTAD's free seaborne dataset is organised by cargo
type, not by region pair. A genuine origin-to-destination flow map cannot be built
from free data at the detail a map needs.

**The answer: chokepoints.** The EIA publishes real, measured oil volumes through
each of the world's maritime chokepoints. This is public, authoritative, specific,
and visually far stronger than arcs between regions — because the whole world's
trade is squeezed through about eight narrow places.

| Chokepoint | Volume | Unit | Period | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strait of Malacca | 23.2 | million b/d | 1H 2025 | M | EIA |
| Strait of Hormuz | 20.9 | million b/d | 1H 2025 | M | EIA |
| Cape of Good Hope | 9.3 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Danish Straits | 4.9 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Suez Canal and SUMED | 4.8 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Bab el-Mandeb | 4.1 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Turkish Straits | 3.6 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Panama Canal | 2.0 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |

**Why this fits Jericho specifically.** These are the routes *everybody* uses — the
opposite of exposing your network. It is the most public geography in the trade.
And it pairs with the voyage-haul fact: the Cape of Good Hope volume exists because
traffic diverted away from Bab el-Mandeb, which is precisely why the average cargo
now travels 400 miles further than in 2018. The map explains a number elsewhere on
the site.

**Caveat to carry on the page:** these are oil and petroleum product volumes, not
all cargo. The map must be titled as oil flows, not as trade in general. Mixed
figures and periods above (1H25 and 2024) would need to be presented on one
consistent basis or individually dated.

## F. RECOMMENDED WORDING

Big number, short title, one sentence. English shown; Spanish to follow once the
figures are approved.

**1.**
> ## 12.7 BILLION TONNES
> **Moved by sea each year**
> The world shipped 12.72 billion tonnes of goods by sea in 2024.

**2.**
> ## 66,781 BILLION
> **Tonne-miles — and rising faster than tonnage**
> Cargo volumes grew 2.2% in 2024. The distance that cargo travelled grew 5.9%, the
> fastest since 2011.
>
> *Expandable:* one tonne-mile is one tonne carried one mile. The average voyage now
> runs 5,245 miles, against 4,831 in 2018.

**3.**
> ## 112,500 VESSELS
> **The world merchant fleet**
> Commercial ships in service at the start of 2025, carrying everything from ore to
> grain to containers.

**4.**
> ## 2.44 BILLION DWT
> **What the fleet can carry**
> Deadweight tonnage is simply how much weight a ship can take on — cargo, fuel and
> stores together.

**5.**
> ## 42.5%
> **Of the world's shipping capacity carries dry bulk**
> Bulk carriers are the largest category of the world fleet. They are the ships that
> move ore, concentrates and scrap.

**6.**
> ## 920 MILLION TEU
> **Containers handled in the world's ports**
> Port container handling in 2024, counting every move through every port — including
> the same box more than once where it transfers between ships.
>
> *Expandable:* a TEU is one twenty-foot container. Because a box is counted again at
> each transhipment hub, this measures port activity rather than how much container
> trade took place.

**7.**
> ## 1.7 BILLION TONNES
> **Iron ore moved by sea**
> Seaborne iron ore trade rose 3.6% in 2024.
>
> *Expandable:* of the 60 million tonne increase, 59.1 million tonnes was China.

**8.**
> ## 27.4 MILLION TONNES
> **Refined copper produced in a year**
> More than came out of the world's copper mines in the same year — 22.96 million
> tonnes — because scrap feeds the refineries too.

**9.**
> ## 3,672 TONNES
> **Gold mined in 2025**
> An all-time high, on the World Gold Council's own estimate — which it is careful to
> call an estimate, because the figure is still subject to revision.

**10.**
> ## US$26.26 TRILLION
> **The value of world merchandise trade**
> Every physical good crossing a border in 2025, up 7% on the year before.
>
> *Expandable:* trade in fuels and mining products fell 4% over the same period, and
> fuels alone fell 10%, while total trade rose. Commodity values and trade volumes do
> not move together.

## G. INTERACTION PER FACT

Restrained, as agreed. The numbers are the focus.

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Entry** | Alternating left and right, about 40px of travel, opacity and position together, roughly 600ms, eased out. Nothing flies across the screen. |
| **Count-up** | Fires every time the fact enters view, as you chose. About 1.2s, ease-out so it decelerates into the final value. Digits held in tabular figures so the number does not jitter in width while counting. |
| **Source marker** | A small superscript indicator beside each number. On click it expands in place to give source, year, exact definition, and the status mark (M/P/E/F/C). Never a popup. |
| **Expandable note** | Where there is a second layer — the China share of iron ore, what dwt means, the transhipment caveat — it opens below the sentence, pushing content down rather than overlaying. |
| **Hover** | Nothing on the numbers themselves. Hover belongs to the source marker and the expand control only. |
| **Map** | Chokepoints as points on the dotted-globe style already agreed, scaled by volume. Hover names the chokepoint and gives its figure and period. No auto-rotation while a visitor is reading it. |
| **Reduced motion** | Count-up disabled, numbers render at final value, entry becomes a simple fade. Every fact fully readable. |
| **Phone** | One fact per screen, entry from alternating sides reduced to a short vertical rise, count-up retained. |
