## STATUS KEY

Used in the tables and required on the page itself.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **M** | Measured / reported statistic from the source organisation |
| **MV** | Measured by commercial vessel tracking (AIS-derived); a commercial product, not an official statistic |
| **P** | Preliminary — the source labels it provisional and expects revision |
| **E** | Estimate — the source states it is an estimate subject to revision |
| **F** | Forecast / projection — a prediction, not a record of what happened |
| **C** | Calculation — derived by arithmetic from other figures |

Your rule was to keep these distinct. Several headline figures are **E**, **P** or
**MV**, not **M**, and the page must not present them as hard official statistics.

### Definitions required on the page

- **Merchant fleet (112,500 vessels).** UNCTAD counts propelled seagoing merchant
  vessels of **100 gross tonnes and above**. It excludes inland waterway vessels,
  fishing vessels, military vessels, yachts, and fixed and mobile offshore platforms
  and barges, with the exception of FPSOs and drillships. *Verified.*
- **Deadweight tonnage.** The total weight a ship can carry — cargo, fuel, stores
  and crew together.
- **TEU.** One twenty-foot container.
- **Cabotage.** Domestic coastal trade within one country. The crude oil figure
  excludes it, so it counts international trade only.

### Unit note on crude oil

You chose barrels per day, as the industry quotes it. The only verified **full-year**
crude-only figure is published in **tonnes** (2,224.2 million, 2025). Converting
tonnes to barrels depends on the density of each particular crude, so any
barrels-per-day figure I derived would be a **calculation (C)**, not a measured
statistic, and would have to be labelled as such.

Three options, your call:
1. **Publish in tonnes** as the source does. Consistent with every other tonnage
   fact on the page, and nothing is derived. *Recommended.*
2. Publish in tonnes and give an approximate barrels-per-day figure in the
   expandable note, marked as a calculation with the density caveat stated.
3. Use the EIA's 79.8 million b/d instead — but that is **first half of 2025 only**
   and covers **crude plus refined products**, so it answers a different question.

## E. WHERE TWO REPUTABLE SOURCES DIFFER

You asked for these to be explained rather than silently resolved.

**Copper, USGS versus ICSG.**

| | USGS *MCS 2026* | ICSG Table 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Mine production 2024 | 23,000 thousand t | 22,955.0 thousand t |
| Mine production 2025 | 23,000 thousand t (est.) | 23,204.8 thousand t (prelim.) |
| Refinery production 2024 | 27,600 thousand t | 27,413.5 thousand t |
| Refinery production 2025 | 29,000 thousand t (est.) | 28,663.1 thousand t (prelim.) |

The differences are small — under 1.5% throughout — and arise from different
reporting cut-offs and country coverage. USGS rounds to the nearest thousand tonnes;
ICSG reports to one decimal and revises monthly.

**Recommendation for the website: use USGS.** It is a government statistical agency,
freely published, cited in one annual document that is easy for a visitor to check,
and its rounding is honest about the precision actually available. ICSG is the better
source for tracking the market month to month, which is not what this page does. The
ICSG figures are kept in **B** so you have them.

**Note on the USGS rounding.** Because USGS gives 23,000 for both 2024 and 2025, the
page must not present "23 million tonnes, 2025" in a way that implies growth. The
flatness is the fact.

## F. THE MAP

You chose real seaborne trade flows between regions. One problem and one good answer.

**The problem.** Region-to-region seaborne flow matrices are commercial products.
Clarksons, Kpler, LSEG and AXSMarine sell them; UNCTAD's free seaborne dataset is
organised by cargo type, not region pair. A genuine origin-to-destination flow map
cannot be built from free data at the detail a map needs.

**The answer: chokepoints.** The EIA publishes real, measured oil volumes through each
of the world's maritime chokepoints. Public, authoritative, specific, and visually far
stronger than arcs between regions — because the whole world's trade is squeezed
through about eight narrow places.

| Chokepoint | Volume | Unit | Period | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strait of Malacca | 23.2 | million b/d | 1H 2025 | M | EIA |
| Strait of Hormuz | 20.9 | million b/d | 1H 2025 | M | EIA |
| Cape of Good Hope | 9.3 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Danish Straits | 4.9 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Suez Canal and SUMED | 4.8 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Bab el-Mandeb | 4.1 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Turkish Straits | 3.6 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |
| Panama Canal | 2.0 | million b/d | 2024 | M | EIA |

**Why this fits Jericho specifically.** These are the routes *everybody* uses — the
opposite of exposing your network. It is the most public geography in the trade. And
it pairs with the voyage-haul fact: the Cape of Good Hope volume exists because
traffic diverted away from Bab el-Mandeb, which is precisely why the average cargo now
travels 400 miles further than in 2018. The map explains a number elsewhere on the
site.

**Caveats to carry on the page.** These are oil and petroleum product volumes, not all
cargo, so the map must be titled as oil flows rather than trade in general. The periods
are mixed — 1H 2025 for two, 2024 for the rest — so either present them on one
consistent basis or date each individually.

## H. INTERACTION PER FACT

Restrained, as agreed. The numbers are the focus.

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Entry** | Alternating left and right, about 40px of travel, opacity and position together, roughly 600ms, eased out. Nothing flies across the screen. |
| **Count-up** | Fires every time the fact enters view, as you chose. About 1.2s, ease-out so it decelerates into the final value. Tabular figures so the number does not jitter in width while counting. |
| **Source marker** | A small superscript indicator beside each number. On click it expands in place to give source, year, exact definition, and the status mark. Never a popup. |
| **Status mark** | Shown inside the expanded source note, in words rather than letters: "measured statistic", "provisional", "estimate — subject to revision by the source", "commercial vessel-tracking data". |
| **Expandable note** | Where there is a second layer — Australia's iron ore share, the Arabian Gulf share, the transhipment caveat — it opens below the sentence, pushing content down rather than overlaying. |
| **Hover** | Nothing on the numbers themselves. Hover belongs to the source marker and the expand control only. |
| **Map** | Chokepoints as points on the dotted-globe style already agreed, scaled by volume. Hover names the chokepoint and gives its figure and period. No auto-rotation while a visitor is reading. |
| **Reduced motion** | Count-up disabled, numbers render at final value, entry becomes a simple fade. Every fact fully readable. |
| **Phone** | One fact per screen, side entry reduced to a short vertical rise, count-up retained. |
