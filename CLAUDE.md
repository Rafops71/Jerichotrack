# Working on JerichoTrack

## When you hit a wall, or want a second opinion, say so

Rafael's standing instruction, 01/10/2026.

Do not quietly pick a way through a decision you are not confident about, and do
not go silent while stuck. Stop and tell him, and write out a prompt he can paste
to another AI or show to someone else.

The prompt must stand on its own — whoever reads it has none of this
conversation — and must contain:

1. **The problem**, with enough background to make sense cold.
2. **What you think and why**, stated plainly as your opinion rather than fact.
3. **The options**, including the one you favour and what each costs.
4. **A request for alternatives or corrections**, and for anything they would do
   differently.

Say honestly where your confidence actually sits. "I think A, about 60/40 over
B" is useful; a confident tone over a coin-flip is not.

### When this applies

- You are genuinely stuck, or going round in circles.
- You are about to make a call that is hard to undo.
- You are about to do something that normally counts as a bad idea, even when
  you believe this case is the exception. Rewriting a failing test so it passes,
  widening a timeout, loosening a check, deleting something that looks unused.
  Being able to argue it is justified is exactly what someone making the mistake
  would also do.
- Two reasonable approaches exist and the choice is really the owner's: how the
  app should behave, what the checklist should say, what something should look
  like.

### When it does not

Ordinary work with an obvious answer. Do not manufacture dilemmas, and do not
use this to avoid deciding things that are plainly yours to decide. One real
question beats five invented ones.

### Also

Ask before choices that are the owner's rather than yours, instead of deciding
and reporting afterwards. He has asked for this more than once.
